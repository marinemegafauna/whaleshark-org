sections:
  - id: range
    heading: The largest fish
    body: |
      Whale sharks reach 18–20 m in total length. They live in tropical and warm-temperate oceans, in both coastal and oceanic waters, and have been recorded to at least 1,928 m depth.
  - id: subpopulations
    heading: Two distinct subpopulations
    body: |
      Genetic evidence separates whale sharks into Atlantic and Indo-Pacific subpopulations. The Atlantic holds about 25% of the global population and the Indo-Pacific about 75%.

      The species is strongly segregated by size and sex. Coastal aggregation sites are mostly used by juvenile males. The largest known sites host hundreds to low thousands of individuals.
  - id: movement
    heading: Mobile, with familiar places
    body: |
      Individuals are highly mobile, but many also show site fidelity. A shark may travel widely and still return to the same aggregation site.
  - id: threats
    heading: Threats
    body: |
      Incidental catch in net fisheries and vessel strikes are major threats. Vessel strikes can cause serious injury and inferred mortality. Whale shark meat is consumed locally and fins are likely to enter trade.

      Target fisheries operated historically in the Indo-Pacific. Most closed during the 1990s and 2000s.
  - id: status
    heading: Endangered
    body: |
      The 2025 IUCN Red List reassessment classifies the whale shark as **Endangered (A2bd)**. It infers a global decline of 50–79% over three generations, or 120 years. The estimated Indo-Pacific decline is greater than 65%, meeting the Endangered threshold; the Atlantic decline is at least 30%, meeting the Vulnerable threshold.

      The species was assessed as Endangered in 2016, Vulnerable in 2005 and 2000, and Data Deficient in 1996.
  - id: photo-id
    heading: Why photo-ID matters
    body: |
      Every whale shark's spot pattern is unique. Identified individuals are how researchers estimate aggregation sizes, movements, growth and survival. Scars and injuries recorded to a shared standard are how vessel-strike and fishing-gear impacts are measured across sites.

      Read [how photo-identification works](/how-it-works), or [drop a photo](/#drop-photo) to add an observation.
